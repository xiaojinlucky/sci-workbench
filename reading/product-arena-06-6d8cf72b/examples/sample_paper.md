# Illustrative Example: Copper-Indium Alloy Catalysts for CO2 Electroreduction

## Abstract
Electrochemical carbon dioxide reduction (CO2RR) converts waste CO2 into value-added fuels. However, copper catalysts suffer from poor selectivity toward carbon monoxide (CO) due to competing hydrogen evolution reaction (HER). Here we show that an oxide-derived copper-indium alloy (CuIn-OD) achieves 88.5% Faradaic efficiency (FE) for CO at -0.7 V vs RHE in 0.1 M KHCO3.

## Introduction
Although copper-based electrodes are widely studied for CO2RR, controlling product selectivity remains challenging because surface intermediates favor multiple competing pathways. Here we develop a galvanic-replacement CuIn-OD catalyst and evaluate its selectivity across n = 18 independent electrode replicates (Fig. 1).

## Methods
Catalysts were synthesized by galvanic replacement on porous Cu foam and tested in a three-electrode H-cell containing CO2-saturated 0.1 M KHCO3 (pH 6.8, 25 °C) with n = 18 electrodes. Bare oxide-derived Cu (Cu-OD) and argon-purged blank electrolytes served as controls to exclude carbon contamination from the cell housing.

## Results
Fig. 2a shows that CuIn-OD reached 88.5 ± 1.4% FE_CO at -0.7 V vs RHE compared with 31.2 ± 2.1% for the Cu-OD control (p < 0.001). In argon-purged blank controls, CO production remained below 0.2%, ruling out electrolyte decomposition as the carbon source.
Fig. 2: Selectivity comparison across potentials (n = 6 per potential; error bars show s.d.), indicating that In surface segregation suppresses HER kinetics.

## Discussion
The improved CO selectivity aligns with weakened *H binding on indium-enriched facets. A key limitation of this study is that durability tests were confined to 12 h in batch H-cells rather than industrial membrane electrode assemblies (MEAs); long-term stability under high current density (>200 mA/cm^2) remains to be tested.
